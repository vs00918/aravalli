### 3. RBI 'On Tap' Licensing Norms for Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs)
**Priority:** P1 — Deep  
**Category:** Banking Regulation & Co-operatives  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Why Important:** Establishes the entry gateway for large credit cooperative societies to convert into full-fledged UCBs.

**Key Eligibility & Prudential Criteria**
- **Operating Track Record**: Minimum **10 years** of continuous operations.
- **Deposit Base**: Minimum **₹10,000 crore** audited deposit base.
- **Net Worth**: Minimum **₹300 crore**.
- **Capital Adequacy (CRAR)**: Minimum **12%**.
- **Asset Quality**: Net NPA not exceeding **3%**.
- **Track Record**: Positive and progressive trend in operational/financial performance over the preceding **5 years**.
- **Phase 1 Scope**: Restricted to societies registered under the **Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002** with multi-state geographic diversification.
- **Governance Cap**: Individual promoter/member shareholding capped at **5%**.

**Understand It**
- *Why is RBI opening on-tap UCB licenses?* Large multi-state credit societies hold systemic public deposits without bank-grade supervision. Converting eligible ones into UCBs brings them under direct RBI regulation, CRAR norms, and DICGC insurance cover.

**Exam Angle**
- 5-statement question on numerical thresholds: 10 yrs track record, ₹10k cr deposits, ₹300 cr net worth, 12% CRAR, 3% Net NPA, 5% shareholding cap.

### 4. RBI Scale-Based Regulation: NBFC Upper Layer (NBFC-UL) List 2026–27
**Priority:** P1 — Deep  
**Category:** Banking & NBFC Regulation  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Why Important:** Upper Layer NBFCs face bank-like prudential regulations and mandatory public listing requirements.

**Key Facts & Regulatory Architecture**
- **Tata Sons Private Limited** classified as an **Upper-Layer NBFC (NBFC-UL)** under principle-based identification (standalone assets $>₹2$ trillion).
- **Total Upper Layer NBFCs**: **17 entities** identified for 2026–27.
- **Government Entities in NBFC-UL**: REC Ltd, Power Finance Corporation (PFC), Indian Railway Finance Corporation (IRFC), HUDCO.
- **5-Year Lock-In Rule**: Once classified as NBFC-UL, enhanced regulatory requirements apply for **at least 5 years**, even if the entity fails the asset/scoring criteria in subsequent years (e.g., PNB Housing Finance, Sammaan Capital).
- **Mandatory Listing**: Upper Layer NBFCs must mandatorily list on stock exchanges within **3 years** of classification.

**NBFC Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) Layer Hierarchy**
```
┌─────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Layer           │ Regulatory Asset / Structure Threshold                 │
├─────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Base Layer      │ Non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size < ₹1,000 cr   │
│ Middle Layer    │ Non-deposit taking ≥ ₹1,000 cr + All Deposit-taking    │
│ Upper Layer     │ Top 10 scoring / Assets > ₹1 lakh cr (Bank-like norms) │
│ Top Layer       │ Substantial systemic risk (Populated at RBI's choice)  │
└─────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Exam Angle**
- Listing timeframe (`3 years`), Upper Layer lock-in (`5 years`), Base/Middle layer asset cutoff (`₹1,000 crore`).

### 5. D-SIB Framework & RBI Leverage Ratio Buffer (Effective April 1, 2027)
**Priority:** P1 — Deep  
**Category:** Banking Regulation & Basel III  
**Why Important:** Protects systemic financial stability and aligns domestic capital requirements with Basel III standards.

**Key Facts**
- **Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs)**:
  - **State Bank of India (SBI)**: Bucket 4 $\to$ **0.80%** additional CET1 surcharge.
  - **HDFC Bank**: Bucket 2 $\to$ **0.40%** additional CET1 surcharge.
  - **ICICI Bank**: Bucket 1 $\to$ **0.20%** additional CET1 surcharge.
- **Minimum Leverage Ratio**: Unchanged at **4.0% for D-SIBs** and **3.5% for all other commercial banks**.
- **G-SIB Branches in India**: Must maintain 3.5% + applicable G-SIB leverage ratio buffer.
- **Derivative Exposure Multiplier**: Calculated as $1.4 \times (\text{Replacement Cost} + \text{Potential Future Exposure})$.

### 6. RBI Credit Valuation Adjustment (CVA) Risk Capital Framework
**Priority:** P1 — Memorize  
**Category:** Risk Management & Basel Framework  
**Proposed Effective Date:** 1 April 2027  
**Why Important:** Replaces 2011 norms on derivative-related counterparty credit risk capital charges.

**Key Provisions**
- Applies to all commercial banks except SFBs, Payments Banks, and LABs.
- **Basic Approach for CVA (BA-CVA)**: Full BA-CVA (recognizes hedges) and Reduced BA-CVA (no hedges).
- **Simplified Approach**: Available for banks with non-centrally cleared derivatives up to **₹10 trillion** (CVA capital = 100% of counterparty credit risk capital).
- **Risk Weights**: Sovereigns = 0.5% (Investment Grade) / 2% (others); Financial Sector = 5% (IG) / 12% (others).

### 7. RBI Loan Recovery Directions & Financed Smartphone Lock Norms
**Priority:** P1 — Deep  
**Category:** Consumer Protection & Digital Lending  
**Effective Date:** 1 January 2027 (Extended from 1 October 2026)  
**Why Important:** First comprehensive statutory charter protecting retail borrowers from coercive recovery and digital device lockouts.

**Key Operational Rules**
- **Agent Calling Hours**: Strictly between **8:00 AM and 7:00 PM**. Contact during bereavement or medical emergencies is prohibited.
- **Mandatory Agent Certification**: All recovery agents must be certified by the **Indian Institute of Banking and Finance (IIBF)** (1-year compliance window for newly covered REs).
- **Technology Restrictions on Financed Mobile Phones**:
  - No restriction allowed until the loan is **30 Days Past Due (DPD)** despite notices.
  - Complete device restrictions permitted only after **60 DPD**.
  - **Protected Functions**: Banks/apps can **NEVER** disable incoming calls, SMS services, or emergency SOS calling.
  - **Restoration**: Device must be unlocked within **1 hour** of bill payment.
  - **Bank Penalty for Delay**: **₹250 per hour** of delay, capped at total loan amount.

**Descriptive Use:** High (Financial consumer protection, ethical debt collection, digital lending guardrails).

### 11. Credit Guarantee Scheme for Microfinance Institutions 2.0 (CGSMFI 2.0)
**Priority:** P1 — Deep  
**Category:** Microfinance & Priority Sector  
**Administering Agency:** National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company Ltd (NCGTC)  

**New Lending & Guarantee Norms**
- **Small & Mid-Size MFI Sub-quota**: Banks must lend at least **15%** of their total ₹20,000 crore corpus to small and mid-size MFIs (earlier 5% and 10% separate sub-targets).
- **MFI Categorisation by Assets Under Management (AUM)**:
  - *Small MFI*: AUM $< ₹500$ crore (Max bank loan capped at ₹100 crore).
  - *Medium MFI*: AUM ₹500 crore to $< ₹2,000$ crore (Max bank loan capped at ₹200 crore).
  - *Large MFI*: AUM $> ₹2,000$ crore (Max bank loan capped at ₹1,000 crore).
- **Sanction Cap**: Bank loans capped at **20% of the MFI's AUM**, subject to the absolute caps above.

### 12. IRDAI Master Reforms: Intermediaries & Insurer Investment Norms
**Priority:** P1 — Deep  
**Category:** Insurance Regulation  
**Regulator:** IRDAI (HQ: Hyderabad | Chairperson: Ajay Seth)  

**Key Provisions**
- **Perpetual Registration**: Certificates of registration for intermediaries remain valid indefinitely subject to payment of annual fee (higher of **₹10,000** or **0.04% of commissions/receipts**).
- **Mandatory Training**: Insurance marketing firm salespersons must complete **25 hours of training every 3 years**.
- **Insurer Investment Limits**:
  - Up to **20%** in operational Infrastructure Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) rated AA or higher.
  - Combined investment in private equity, AIFs, and VCFs: up to **3% for Life Insurers** and **5% for General Insurers**.
  - Promoter group exposure capped at **5% of investment assets**.
  - Repo and G-Sec lending transactions capped at **25% of available securities or ₹10,000 crore**, whichever is lower.
